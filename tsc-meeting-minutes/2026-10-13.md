### Meeting Notes

* **Meeting Time Adjustment and Welcome:**   
  * Jason opened the TSC meeting held in a new time slot intended to accommodate participants from the APAC region, welcoming Tim who joined from Japan. Tim and J.R. noted the importance of the adjusted schedule for global attendance.  
  * Committee members and conducted brief introductions.  
* **RSAC Talk Submissions and Guidelines:**  
  * Jason announced that the RSA Conference talk submission deadline was extended to October 15th, noting that approximately 10 ideas are currently tracked on a shared spreadsheet. Shrey inquired about formatting and marketing requirements for submissions, and Jason clarified that presenters do not require formal approval for independent topics, though aligning presentations with CoSAI deliverables is strongly encouraged, with J.R. adding that reflecting workstream efforts provides valuable cross-industry support.  
* **TSC Project Approvals:**  
  * Jason reported that the calls for consensus on the GraphRAG project and the testable risk map project plan from WS2, which involve collaboration with Duke University and cybersecurity students, passed with no objections from the TSC. Josiah added that a soft launch has been initiated with 14 Duke students and two other CoSAI members volunteering to contribute to each project.  
* **WS3 Overview and Risk Map Updates:**   
  * Shrey presented a review of WS3 focusing on risk and governance, detailing that the workstream previously operated three special interest groups. For the CoSAI Risk Map (CoSAI-RM), Shrey explained that the team expanded version 1 to encompass 26 different AI system components, numerous risks, controls, 10 personas, and six external framework definitions. Shrey noted that markdown files and agent instructions were added to facilitate contributions, alongside integrating details from WS4's MCP paper. Zeal added that these concrete controls enable practitioners to codify solutions using agents, while acknowledging that user education regarding GitHub remains necessary.  
* **Shared Responsibility Framework and Risk Map Integration:**   
  * Shrey and Zeal discussed the shared responsibility framework initiated by WS2, which was transferred into WS3 to integrate directly into the risk map. Zeal explained that the framework maps the four risk map components against five AI operating models (AI software as a service, AI platform as a service, agent platform as a service, bring-your-own-model, and self-deployed models) and nine personas from the risk map to clarify provider versus consumer responsibilities.  
  * Zeal shared a spreadsheet demonstrating the matrix layout that bridges the operating models and nine risk map personas to define external and internal responsibilities across system layers. She requested feedback from members to simplify the matrix for broader business and practitioner consumption, noting its utility for evaluating tools and establishing security guardrails.  
* **Codeguard and Security for AI-Assisted Code Development:**  
  * Shrey reviewed the security for AI-assisted code development SIG, originally project Codeguard donated by Cisco. Shrey stated that the team standardized secure coding rules to be model agnostic, released sub-agents to perform security reviews using those rules, and developed an evaluation harness using Inspect AI. Shrey outlined upcoming goals, including publishing evaluation results, evaluating Codeguard with local models to assist enterprises with token economics and cost reduction, and progressing a threat modeling draft paper for security review agents through the TSC.  
* **WS3 Lessons Learned on AI Tooling and Engagement:**   
  * Responding to a question from Jason, Shrey highlighted that detailed initial pull requests and issues labeled as good first issues successfully aided onboarding, while fluctuating industry contributions over six-month intervals presented an ongoing challenge. Zeal and Shrey noted that maintaining momentum was hindered when moving outside of GitHub into static PDF formats. Regarding AI assistants, Shrey shared that custom instructions reduced generated filler, but human oversight remains critical because agents frequently miscalculate severity ratings, requiring triage boards managed by human contributors. Jodi requested a documented summary of these tooling successes and challenges for other workstreams.  
* **TSC Issues Review and Cleanup:**   
  * Jason led a review of open GitHub issues.  
  * J.R. requested that issue closures be routed through the co-chairs while encouraging members to update status offline.
