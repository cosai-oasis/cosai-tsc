* **Blog Post on Autonomous Agent Threat Behaviors and Recommendations:**   
  * Ian presented a pull request for a short blog-like article addressing the problem of increasingly capable models acting autonomously, probing boundaries, and attempting to break out to achieve their goals. Ian explained that the article outlines prior incidents, discusses how agents actively try to commit unauthorized actions, and offers tangible recommendations including sandboxing, bounded authority, and isolation. This article is a follow-up to an action item from the previous TSC meeting.  
  * David L. proposed shifting the blog's perspective to assume that advanced threat actors are more capable than the defenders, making the primary goal not prevention, but making attacks extraordinarily noisy, expensive, and reducing the time to detect breaches from hours to seconds. Ian expressed strong agreement with emphasizing detection and recovery over prevention.   
  * David L. agreed to provide specific suggested input to strengthen the text regarding detection windows without expanding the paper excessively.  
* **Approval Timeline for the Blog Post:**   
  * Claudia stated that marketing-focused technical blog posts require a three-day review period and call for consensus on the mailing list. The group agreed to set Thursday end of day as the deadline for the consensus review on the mailing list to allow publishing the following week.  
* **Proposal for a Consensus Industry Framework for AI Jailbreaks:**   
  * J.R. introduced a discussion on a consensus industry framework for artificial intelligence jailbreaks, referencing a blog post detailing four severity criteria: capability gain, breadth of capability gain, ease of weaponization, and discoverability. J.R. suggested exploring whether CoSAI should pursue a standard or framework in this area to unify closed and open model communities.   
  * David L. emphasized the need for crisp framing and partnerships with other organizations before holding a vote. J.R. recommended that interested members develop a one-page write-up outlining what CoSAI could do, consulting with CoSAI experts, to eventually house the effort within a special interest group or workstream. Josiah Hagen agreed to take the topic back to WS2 for discussion during their next meeting.  
* **Telemetry Paper GitHub Practices and Roadmap Mapping:**   
  * Josiah raised questions regarding the telemetry paper, asking about the threshold for moving initial drafts to GitHub and whether the document should map to the current or future state of the CoSAI Risk Map framework. David L. advised mapping to upcoming components and risks and controls that are already completed and ready for review rather than speculative future work.   
  * J.R. and David L. supported a GitHub-first approach with a low barrier to entry, emphasizing that drafts must be properly annotated and labeled as work-in-progress to distinguish them from fully approved documents.  
* **CoSAI-RM Component Rework and Feedback Challenges:**   
  * David L. highlights the significant difficulty they face in getting people to lean in and provide feedback on proposed component reworks necessary for landing MCP risks and controls. He explained that within CoSAI, risks link to controls, and controls link to risks and components, with components acting as base items for aggregation. This component establishment is a prerequisite before WS4 can review the decomposition of their paper into controls and risks. Despite performing extensive updates to fix the tools and agent areas and adding identity components, David has received zero comments and finds that tagging individuals in GitHub is entirely ineffective. He is seeking alternative pathways to gather necessary feedback without having to repeatedly rely on announcements in TSC meetings.  
  * Addressing the lack of engagement with asynchronous GitHub tags, Josiah suggested scheduling longer meetings with explicit feedback-gathering sessions. Debadatta proposed dividing three to four participants into smaller groups to review materials and collect feedback before coming back together to share. David L. expressed appreciation for both suggestions and committed to utilizing a mixture of these approaches moving forward to improve participation and feedback collection.
