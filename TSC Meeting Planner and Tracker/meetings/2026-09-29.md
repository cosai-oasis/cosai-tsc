## 4. CoSAI Week in Review

> A summary of what was discussed across CoSAI Workstreams and SIGs
> since the last TSC meeting. Sourced from meeting minutes files dated
> within the last 14 days. Groups that did not meet are noted explicitly.

| Group | Last Met | Summary |
|---|---|---|
| WS1 — Software Supply Chain Security for AI Systems | Did not meet | Did not meet |
| WS2 — Preparing Defenders for a Changing Cybersecurity Landscape | 2026-09-22 | The group reviewed TSC feedback on the telemetry paper's comprehensive scope and discussed reducing the initial publication to focus on OCSF and OpenTelemetry. Josiah Hagen introduced two new student-driven projects pending TSC approval: a graph MCP server acting as a CoSAI oracle (with Duke University master's students), and a testable risk-map harness using 49 documented attacks from the telemetry paper. The jailbreak scoping discussion proposed categorizing capability consequence levels across zero trust domains, with a focus on entire AI systems rather than individual LLMs. Arthur Saputkin was tasked with drafting a scoping statement for AI telemetry security to submit to the TSC for formal approval — both student projects require TSC mandate before proceeding. |
| Telemetry SIG — AI Security Telemetry (WS2) | 2026-09-24 | The group reviewed TSC feedback on the telemetry paper, agreeing to retitle it "Security Telemetry for Agentic AI Systems" and to add event-type categorization (traces, metrics, configuration states). Benedict Lau from the WS4 Trust Graph group presented the trust graph initiative, identifying strong alignment with WS2 telemetry work around delegation records and agent telemetry; the group discussed incorporating trust graph scope into the paper's antiscope. Bill Stout proposed conducting a prior-art SWOT analysis before launching new workstream projects. The group continues to address TSC feedback on paper scope, title, and threat context. |
| WS3 — AI Security Risk Governance | Did not meet | Did not meet |
| WS3 Threat Modeling — Agentic Coding Threat Modeling (WS3) | 2026-09-25 | The group refined risks A6 and A7 in the agentic coding threat modeling document (currently in Google Drive pending final GitHub migration) and discussed mitigation strategies for compromised AI reviewers. Kevin Calloway reported strong interest from the ADLC group after presenting the development loop framework, with an extended presentation planned. The group debated their next project focus — high-risk offensive security/red teaming for frontier models versus general agentic coding — and agreed to survey other workstreams to align on industry priority. New contributor Navneet Keshav (Lenovo) joined, bringing agentic threat modeling expertise and introducing the Maestro framework as an alternative to STRIDE. |
| CoSAI-RM SIG — Coalition for Secure AI Risk Map | 2026-09-23 | David LaBianca scoped the V1 release of the SAFE framework through the MCP V1 paper and PR 507, with a rewritten component graphing system to improve readability. The primary blocker is PR 507 (46 new controls and risks requiring full human review); the group agreed to schedule at least seven live one-hour review sessions three times weekly. David LaBianca noted a contingency plan to pull WS4 leads Ian and Sarah plus a TSC lead into an intensive review session if attendance is insufficient — this is a potential TSC ask. Additional risks from WS2 are being integrated and agent evaluation CI infrastructure is being established. |
| Code SIG — Security of AI-Assisted Code Generation | 2026-09-22 | The group addressed technical harness issues by increasing turn limits and time limits for CodeGuard. The Windsurf-to-Devon rebranding was merged to main, with a coordinated release pending larger CodeGuard updates. Thomas Bartlett is tracking a planned technical report or blog post on CodeGuard and harness results (discussed with Shrey), which may eventually surface as a TSC-level deliverable. Public scoring scripts are planned to enable independent use of harness results. |
| WS4 — Secure Design Patterns for Agentic Systems | 2026-09-17 | Laura Seletos (NVIDIA) proposed a standardized security patterns framework providing vendor-agnostic prescriptive guidance and reusable architecture artifacts for AI workloads, intended to evolve into infrastructure-as-code hosted in a CoSAI GitHub library. The containment follow-on paper is progressing with multiple PRs submitted; Ian Molloy flagged review bottlenecks. ODIS was reframed into three architectural layers (bridge, passport, router). Ian Molloy raised concern about internal consistency across CoSAI deliverables and ongoing difficulty recruiting volunteers for risk mapping — the TSC was noted as a potential escalation path for PR review bottlenecks. |
| ADLC SIG — Security of Agent Development Lifecycle | 2026-09-23 | Emrick Donadei led review of the decommissioning RFC, which is on track for submission to Yan and Sara after incorporating feedback including a new formal definition for "adaptive agent." The agent behavioral monitoring RFC was deferred for additional feedback on practical enforcement methods. Artifact review timelines were pushed out by two weeks. Kevin Calloway presented the code threat modeling framework, generating interest in cross-group SDLC collaboration — a follow-up presentation is planned. The ADLC paper (paper #197) is under group review with paper ownership being clarified. |
| Agent Credentials Group | 2026-09-24 | The group completed a terminology cleanup of section 5 (renaming "tokens" to "credentials" throughout) and moved section 5.7 to an appendix table. The team agreed to maintain the practitioner white paper format with technical specs in the appendix and to add a glossary. Section 4 will be restructured to introduce identity patterns before use cases, per Jeremy's recommendation. The group transitioned to an asynchronous workflow to produce a clean version 2 before the next review cycle; this paper targets an October 31 deadline. |
| Multimodal Agentic Security — Multimodal Threat Taxonomy (WS4) | 2026-09-25 | Shriti Priya committed to delivering a modality-independent taxonomy by October 31, with a presentation to WS4 for feedback targeting the following week (an RFC submission by end of October). The group evaluated the taxonomy against 85 attack examples and identified gaps in cross-modal and multimodal streaming scenarios. The team agreed to separate the taxonomy and threat modeling documents into sequential phases. Klaudia Krawiecka (Meta) and Alan Messer (EY) joined as new contributors. Physical AI was confirmed in scope; task responsibilities were divided among Shriti Priya, Kevin Calloway, and Navneet Keshav. The October 31 deadline aligns with the TSC-tracked roadmap milestone. |
| Trust Graph — Agent Trust Graph (WS4) | 2026-09-24 | The group discussed consolidating with the WS2 Defenders telemetry team, noting approximately 75% content overlap, and prepared a presentation for WS2 to float becoming a subgroup. The two core focus areas are agent telemetry (trust graph API, log infrastructure, signed bundle export) and records of delegation (agent charters, delegation chains, schema). Benedict Lau committed to requesting a 10-minute slot at the WS2 meeting to present; a slide deck was to be generated using Claude from Miro board materials. The structural question of whether to merge into WS2 remains open and may eventually require TSC visibility. |
